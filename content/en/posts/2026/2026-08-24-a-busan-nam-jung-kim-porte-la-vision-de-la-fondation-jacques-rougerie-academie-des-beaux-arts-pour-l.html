From August 17 to 24, 2026, the Korea Maritime & Ocean University in Busan, South Korea, hosted the 10th edition of the International Ocean Architecture Design Workshop,
on the theme "Floating Architecture: Future Housing and Ocean Infrastructure Design."
On this occasion, Nam Jung Kim, HMONP architect, member of the Université de Lorraine-CREM and Junior Ambassador of the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts, took part in the workshop as design tutor and coordinator.
Bringing together students, architects, researchers, teachers, and professionals from Korea, Japan, China, and France, this international week made it possible to bring together different approaches and cultures around new forms of architecture linked to marine environments.Lectures, design workshops, discussions, and critique sessions marked this week devoted to the issues of housing and ocean infrastructure.
As a member of the organizing committee, Nam Jung Kim helped support the participants in developing their projects. As a Junior Ambassador of the Foundation, he also presented his mission and vision, based on dialogue between architecture, science, technology, and the ocean, to imagine new ways of inhabiting and coexisting with marine environments.
During the workshop, four architectural projects were developed around new forms of housing, mobility, and floating infrastructure. These proposals made it possible to explore new relationships between human societies and marine ecosystems, while questioning models of habitat and planning in the face of environmental transformations.
In connection with the themes of the Foundation's International Competition, this initiative provided a space for reflection and experimentation for a new generation of architects and designers. It illustrates the importance of international exchanges in bringing forth innovative responses to the major challenges at the intersection of architecture, science, technology, and the ocean.
