From October 15 to 18, 2026, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts took part in the first edition of the Rencontres de Rochefort, organized by the Corderie Royale - Centre International de la Mer and conceived by its President, Olivier Poivre d'Arvor. Meetings, debates, visits, and screenings explored the link between Land and Sea, at the crossroads of climate change adaptation and tensions over water resources. From October 15 to 18, 2026, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts took part in the first edition of the Rencontres de Rochefort, organized by the Corderie Royale - Centre International de la Mer and conceived by its President, Olivier Poivre d'Arvor.
Meetings, debates, visits, and screenings explored the link between Land and Sea, at the crossroads of climate change adaptation and tensions over water resources.
Jacques Rougerie took part in these discussions alongside the Minister of the Sea, Catherine Chabaud, scientists, elected officials, ANEL, associations, and citizens, with one conviction: imagining resilient coastlines also means inventing new ways of living with the ocean.
The Foundation warmly thanks Olivier Poivre d'Arvor and the entire Corderie Royale team for their welcome. It also salutes the more than 1,000 schoolchildren welcomed, as well as the students of the Lycée de la mer et du littoral de Bourcefranc, whose call was heard: "It is with young people that livable coastal territories will be built."

From October 15 to 18, 2026, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts took part in the first edition of the Rencontres de Rochefort, organized by the Corderie Royale - Centre International de la Mer and conceived by its President, Olivier Poivre d'Arvor.

Meetings, debates, visits, and screenings explored the link between Land and Sea, at the crossroads of climate change adaptation and tensions over water resources. Jacques Rougerie took part in these discussions alongside the Minister of the Sea, Catherine Chabaud, scientists, elected officials, ANEL, associations, and citizens, with one conviction: imagining resilient coastlines also means inventing new ways of living with the ocean.

The Foundation warmly thanks Olivier Poivre d'Arvor and the entire Corderie Royale team for their welcome. It also salutes the more than 1,000 schoolchildren welcomed, as well as the students of the Lycée de la mer et du littoral de Bourcefranc, whose call was heard: "It is with young people that livable coastal territories will be built."
