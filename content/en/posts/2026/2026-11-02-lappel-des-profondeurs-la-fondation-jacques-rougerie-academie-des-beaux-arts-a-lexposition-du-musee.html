From October 14, 2026 to April 4, 2027, the Musée national de la Marine is organizing the exhibition "The Call of the Deep: An Adventure Beneath the Seas, from Antiquity to the Present Day". The  Foundation Jacques Rougerie - Académie des beaux-arts participates with "The Reef of Silence", a project by Yunil Nam, winner of the Grand Prize for Architecture and Innovation for the Sea in 2016, as well as the "Underwater Village", conceived by Jacques Rougerie in 1973.

From October 14, 2026 to April 4, 2027, the Musée national de la Marine is organizing a major exhibition entitled

"

The Call of the Depts:

An Adventure Beneath the Seas, from Antiquity to the Present Day."

It traces the history of humanity's fascination with the abyss and the innovations imagined to explore it.

On this occasion, the

Foundation

Jacques Rougerie - Académie des beaux-arts is proud to participate in
this exhibition through: The Reef of Silence, a project by Yunil Nam, winner of the 2016

Grand Prize for Architecture and Innovation for the Sea. It reflects the ability of the Foundation's International Competition to reveal bold talents and ideas for tomorrow's living environments.
As well as the "submarine village", designed and imagined by Jacques Rougerie in 1973. Fifty years of creativity and innovation for a better understanding of the seabed. At the crossroads of oceanography, architecture and bioinspiredness, this visionary project embodies the conviction that has always guided Jacques Rougerie: " The fate of our civilizations will come from the ocean. »

From October 14, 2026 to April 4, 2027, the Musée national de la Marine is organizing a major exhibition entitled " The Call of the Depts: An Adventure Beneath the Seas, from Antiquity to the Present Day." It traces the history of humanity's fascination with the abyss and the innovations imagined to explore it.

On this occasion, the Foundation Jacques Rougerie - Académie des beaux-arts is proud to participate in this exhibition through:

The Reef of Silence, a project by Yunil Nam, winner of the 2016 Grand Prize for Architecture and Innovation for the Sea. It reflects the ability of the Foundation's International Competition to reveal bold talents and ideas for tomorrow's living environments.

As well as the "submarine village", designed and imagined by Jacques Rougerie in 1973. Fifty years of creativity and innovation for a better understanding of the seabed. At the crossroads of oceanography, architecture and bioinspiredness, this visionary project embodies the conviction that has always guided Jacques Rougerie: "The fate of our civilizations will come from the ocean.»
