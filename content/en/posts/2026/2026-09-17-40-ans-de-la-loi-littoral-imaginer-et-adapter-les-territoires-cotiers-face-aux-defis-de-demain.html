At the Institut de France, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts and ANEL brought together elected officials, parliamentarians, experts, and regional stakeholders on the occasion of the 40th anniversary of the Littoral Law, to open a reflection on the future of coastal territories in the face of environmental and climate transformations.
This meeting takes place in a context where rising waters and extreme climate events call for rethinking the balance between landscape protection, territorial development, and the preservation of natural environments.
Opened by Xavier Darcos, Chancellor of the Institut de France, the morning brought together, around Jacques Rougerie and Jean-Charles Orsucci, presentations by Carlos Moreno, former minister Guy Lengagne, and Maître Guillaume Hannotin.
The discussions addressed the issues related to the evolution of our coastlines and the need to adapt the ways of inhabiting and developing coastal areas. They also highlighted the role of architecture, innovation, and science in supporting these changes and imagining new forms of resilience.
Through this meeting, the Foundation continues its ambition to bring architecture, science, innovation, and nature into dialogue in order to contribute to the emergence of coastlines capable of adapting to the changes ahead, while preserving their attractiveness and quality of life.
This initiative marks the beginning of a joint effort with ANEL and the regions to design the coastlines of tomorrow and bring forth new responses to climate challenges: protecting, adapting, and transforming our coastal areas.

At the Institut de France, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts and ANEL brought together elected officials, parliamentarians, experts, and regional stakeholders on the occasion of the 40th anniversary of the Littoral Law, to open a reflection on the future of coastal territories in the face of environmental and climate transformations.

This meeting takes place in a context where rising waters and extreme climate events call for rethinking the balance between landscape protection, territorial development, and the preservation of natural environments. Opened by Xavier Darcos, Chancellor of the Institut de France, the morning brought together, around Jacques Rougerie and Jean-Charles Orsucci, presentations by Carlos Moreno, former minister Guy Lengagne, and Maître Guillaume Hannotin.

The discussions addressed the issues related to the evolution of our coastlines and the need to adapt the ways of inhabiting and developing coastal areas. They also highlighted the role of architecture, innovation, and science in supporting these changes and imagining new forms of resilience.

Through this meeting, the Foundation continues its ambition to bring architecture, science, innovation, and nature into dialogue in order to contribute to the emergence of coastlines capable of adapting to the changes ahead, while preserving their attractiveness and quality of life.

This initiative marks the beginning of a joint effort with ANEL and the regions to design the coastlines of tomorrow and bring forth new responses to climate challenges: protecting, adapting, and transforming our coastal areas.
