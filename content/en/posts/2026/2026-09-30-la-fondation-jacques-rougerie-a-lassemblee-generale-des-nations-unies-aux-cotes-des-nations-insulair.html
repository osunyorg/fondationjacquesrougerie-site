On the occasion of the 81st United Nations General Assembly and Climate Week in New York, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts took part in a week of meetings devoted to the future of island and coastal territories. Alongside leaders, scientists, architects, and international stakeholders, Jacques Rougerie and Amine Lehna brought forward the issues related to rising waters, climate mobility, and the adaptation of territories, while also continuing, among other things, the Museum for Tuvalu project. These exchanges strengthen the Foundation's commitment to resilient territories and to new generations capable of imagining new ways of living with water.

On the occasion of the 81st United Nations General Assembly and Climate Week in New York, Jacques Rougerie and Amine Lehna, the Foundation's Director of International Relations, took part in a week of meetings devoted to the future of island and coastal territories in the face of the consequences of climate change. Bringing together heads of state and government, leaders of international organizations, scientists, architects, and on-the-ground stakeholders, these exchanges made it possible to bring the issues related to rising waters, climate mobility, and the adaptation of territories to the international level. With Tuvalu, the Foundation notably continued the work undertaken around the museum for Tuvalu, a project conceived to preserve and pass on the heritage, memory, and identity of the archipelago in the face of rising waters.
The Foundation also took part in the Rising Nations Gala Dinner, organized by the Global Centre for Climate Mobility on the occasion of the launch of the Rising Nations Institute. This gathering helped strengthen exchanges with several leaders of island states and international stakeholders working alongside the nations and communities most exposed to climate transformations.
The week was marked by the signing of a partnership between The Explorers Club of New York and Mission Neptune, as well as by the Lewis Mumford Lecture given by Carlos Moreno, Chair of the Foundation's Scientific Council, on his vision of more human, resilient cities based on proximity.
Through these meetings and partnerships, the Foundation continues its commitment to island and coastal territories, bringing architecture, science, innovation, and transmission into dialogue. It also continues to mobilize a new generation of architects, designers, engineers, and researchers to imagine new ways of living with water and inhabiting it.
Alongside the nations and communities on the front line of climate transformations, the Jacques Rougerie Foundation - Académie des beaux-arts thus pursues an essential ambition: to preserve heritage, support the adaptation of territories, and give future generations the means to imagine their future.
